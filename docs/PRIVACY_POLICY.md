## Summary

- SongNotes works fully offline with no account. Your songs, chords, and
  recordings stay on your device.
- Creating an account is optional, and used only to sync your songs across
  your own devices.
- Audio you record (Scratchpad takes, calibration tones) **never leaves your
  device**. It is not uploaded anywhere.
- Song content (lyrics, chords, titles, metadata) is **encrypted on your
  device before it's sent to our sync backend**. We — and our backend
  provider — cannot read it.
- The app does not use analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs of any kind.

## What we collect

### If you never create an account

Nothing leaves your device. Songs, chord voicings, recordings, and app
settings are stored locally in an encrypted database on your phone.

### If you create an account

To create an account, we collect:

- **Email address and password** — used to authenticate you, via our
  authentication provider, Supabase. Your password is never stored or seen
  by us directly; Supabase handles credential storage and verification.

Syncing is manual: nothing is uploaded automatically, not even right after you
sign in. Your songs stay on your device until you tap the Sync button
yourself. Once you do, to sync your songs across your devices, we store:

- **Your song content (lyrics, chords, titles, and metadata like BPM/key/
  tuning)** — but only in encrypted form. Your device encrypts this content
  with a key derived from your account password before sending it. Our
  sync backend (Supabase, see below) stores only the resulting ciphertext —
  it cannot read your lyrics or chords, and neither can we.
- **Account-level metadata needed to sync correctly** — an internal account
  identifier, and timestamps/version numbers used to detect and resolve
  conflicts when you edit the same song on two devices.

We do **not** collect or transmit:

- Microphone audio. Recordings made in the Scratchpad feature, and the
  audio played/captured during calibration, are processed and stored
  entirely on your device and are never uploaded.
- Location data.
- Contacts.
- Photos, camera data, or files outside the app's own data.
- Advertising identifiers.

## Biometric unlock

If you enable fingerprint/face unlock for account access, your device's
biometric sensor confirms your identity using Android's own Keystore/
BiometricPrompt system. **The app never receives, stores, or transmits your
fingerprint or face data** — only a yes/no confirmation from the operating
system.

## Third-party service providers

SongNotes uses the following third-party service to provide account and
sync functionality:

- **Supabase** (authentication and database hosting) — processes your email
  address (for login) and the encrypted song data described above. See
  [Supabase's privacy policy](https://supabase.com/privacy) for how they
  handle data on their infrastructure.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with advertisers or data
brokers.

## Children's privacy

SongNotes is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly
collect personal information from children under 13.

## Security

- Songs and account keys are stored locally using an encrypted database
  (SQLCipher) and Android's Keystore.
- Song content synced to our backend is encrypted on your device before
  it's sent, using a key derived from your account password — the backend
  never has access to the key or the plaintext content.
- All network traffic uses HTTPS/TLS.

### How this was written

This draft was generated by reading the app's actual source code rather
than from a generic template: `AudioEngine`/`RecordingForegroundService`
(mic audio stays local), `SupabaseAuthRepository`/`SupabaseSongsAdapter`
(what's sent to Supabase and in what form), `ContentEnvelope` (the

Verify anything you're unsure of before publishing — this is a starting
draft, not legal advice.

Updated 2026-08-15 for Phase 13 (`docs/handoff/PHASE-13-local-first.md`):
Android became local-first with opt-in, strictly manual sync —
`SongSyncWorker.enqueueOneTime` is now only ever called from a direct user
action (`SyncController.requestSync`), never automatically on sign-in. The
claims above ("optional," "stays on your device") were already written this
way in Phase 11 but are now actually enforced by the code rather than
partially aspirational — Phase 11's `LockedAccountScreen` used to gate the
entire song list behind the account password even for a signed-in user with
no unsynced intent; Phase 13 removed that gate.
